General purpose

Turn an ambiguous system-level claim into subject-specific, graph-grounded statements.

01

The problem

Systems may be called decentralized because data or computation is distributed even when coordination or authority remains centralized. The paper calls the missing transferable definition the Decentralization Problem.

02

The formal response

The ontology separates realization multiplicity δ, vertex support λ, and the anchor-relative count µ of center regions reached by a subject.

03

The analytical response

Each declared subject–anchor pair receives Void Tolerance and Imperviousness values after it crosses the logical boundary µ > 1.

04

The tool

Provide a graph, an evaluation profile, vertex realizations, and center regions; the workbench returns classifications, vectors, aggregates, visualizations, comparisons, and proposition traces.

Paper basis: Abstract; §§II, VII–XI, XII, and XIV.

    1

    Multiplicity, support, and centers

    For subject u, δ(pu) counts realization particulars, λ(pu) counts supporting vertices, and µ(pu,a) counts the completed anchor-center regions intersecting that support.

    Centralized pair
    µ(pu,a) = 1
    Decentralized pair
    µ(pu,a) > 1
    Distributed subject
    λ(pu) > 1
    Dimensionality
    |{(u,a)∈Es : µ>1}|
    2

    Proposition 1 — Void Tolerance

    TL(u,a) = 0if µ(pu,a) = 1TL(u,a) = 1if |Gs| = 0, |Gt| > 1, λ > 1TL(u,a) = exp(−rv² · |Gs|ε / |Gt|)otherwise

    rv counts subject-supporting articulation vertices. |Gs| is the largest combined order outside the subject-prioritized reference component after removal.

    3

    Proposition 2 — Imperviousness

    IL(u,a) = 0if |E(Gt)| = 0 or µ(pu,a) = 1IL(u,a) = 1if re = |Gt| − 1 and λ > 1IL(u,a) = exp(1 − (λ/|Gt|)−ε²/re)otherwise

    re is the minimum number of edge deletions required to compromise a subject-supporting vertex.

    4

    Decentralization vectors and aggregate

    Each declared pair yields d⃗u,a = [TL(u,a), IL(u,a)]T. The profile aggregate is the equal element-wise arithmetic mean.

    A(Es) = (1/|Es|) Σ(u,a)∈Es d⃗u,a

    Like-for-like comparison requires compatible evaluation profiles, center interpretations, parameter assignments, and aggregation assumptions.

    Implementation scope

    • Undirected, simple graphs; self-loops and duplicate edges are rejected.
    • Every evaluation is a fixed-time snapshot.
    • Each declared anchor owns one system-level center family; regions may overlap and uncovered vertices receive singleton completion.
    • Ownership, authority, trust, and governance are editable naming templates, not fixed ontology subclasses.
    • Disconnected graphs are accepted with an explicit component-forest warning.
    • ε > 0 changes pair sensitivity, not aggregate vector weighting.
    • Ontology classes depend on µ, never on arbitrary metric thresholds.
    • Coverage λ/|G| and λ/δ are descriptive visual aids; distribution remains λ > 1.
    • Simulation laws, actor/domain models, jitter, and edge noise are experimental generators—not additions to the ontology.
