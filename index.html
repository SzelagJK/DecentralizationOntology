Graph ontology workbench

Specify what is decentralized before comparing how decentralized it is.

The paper addresses the absence of a formal, transferable, and context-independent definition of decentralization. This workbench applies its graph-based, subject-specific framework to explicit system models.

Based on (Authors anonymized for review) “Defining Decentralization: An Ontological Perspective.”

General purpose

Turn an ambiguous system-level claim into subject-specific, graph-grounded statements.

01

The problem

Systems may be called decentralized because data or computation is distributed even when coordination or authority remains centralized. The paper calls the missing transferable definition the Decentralization Problem.

02

The formal response

The ontology treats decentralization as structural and relational, declares multiple subjects separately, and distinguishes realization multiplicity δ from vertex support λ.

03

The analytical response

Each subject receives Void Tolerance and Imperviousness values, forming a two-dimensional vector that exposes structural and functional vulnerability without replacing the ontology classification.

04

The tool

Provide a graph and subject-specific vertex assignments; the workbench returns classifications, vectors, aggregates, visualizations, comparisons, and proposition-level explanations.

Paper basis: Abstract; §§II, VII–XI, XII, and XIV.

    1

    Projection multiplicity and support

    For subject u, δ(pu) = |Rpu| counts distinct realization particulars and λ(pu) = |Vpu| counts vertices supporting at least one realization.

    Centralized subject
    δ(pu) = 1
    Decentralized subject
    δ(pu) > 1
    Distributed subject
    λ(pu) > 1
    Dimensionality
    |Ud(s)|
    2

    Proposition 1 — Void Tolerance

    TL = 0if δ = 1TL = 1if |Gs| = 0, |Gt| > 1, λ > 1TL = exp(−rv² · |Gs|ε / |Gt|)otherwise

    rv counts subject-supporting articulation vertices. |Gs| is the maximum combined order outside the reference component after removal.

    3

    Proposition 2 — Imperviousness

    IL = 0if |E| = 0 or δ = 1IL = 1if re = |Gt| − 1 and λ > 1IL = exp(1 − (λ/|Gt|)−ε²/re)otherwise

    re is the minimum incident-edge count required to isolate any supporting vertex, matching the worked examples.

    4

    Decentralization vectors and aggregate

    Each subject yields d⃗u = [TL, IL]. The multi-dimensional aggregate is the element-wise arithmetic mean.

    A(U) = (1/|U|) Σu∈U d⃗u

    Like-for-like comparison uses vector length only with respect to the same declared subjects and equal treatment of included dimensions.

    Implementation scope

    • Undirected, simple graphs; self-loops and duplicate edges are rejected.
    • Every evaluation is a fixed-time snapshot.
    • Disconnected graphs are accepted with an explicit component-forest warning.
    • ε > 0 changes proposition sensitivity, not aggregate vector weighting.
    • Ontology classes never depend on arbitrary metric thresholds.
    • Coverage λ/|G| and dispersion efficiency λ/δ are descriptive visual aids; the paper’s distribution rule remains λ > 1.
    • Simulation probability laws, placement relations, jitter, and edge noise are experimental generators—not additions to the ontology.
